How it works

Four rules of this world.

You're born at random

Weighted by where babies are actually born today. Your country sets your wages, rent, house prices and how long you'll probably live. Your family decides who pays for university.

Choose first, see later

Like real life, you choose knowing only the prices, the pay and the fees. Afterwards, a hindsight card shows how every option typically turned out across 40 simulated futures, so you learn why a crypto bet and an index fund are not the same. Easy mode shows it before you choose; Hard mode saves it all for the end.

Every choice gets a line

Your passbook gives each decision its own running total that updates every year. Your degree starts in the red, then you watch it turn green and pay for itself.

You race your autopilot twin

Your twin has the same birth and the same luck, but always takes the default option. The gap between your line and theirs is what your choices were worth.

Where the numbers come from

Wages, house prices, university fees and life expectancy are rough figures based on public World Bank and UN data. All money is in dollars adjusted to local prices (PPP) and kept in today's money, so inflation is already taken out.

In the game, stocks grow about 4–5% a year after inflation on average, with real crashes and booms.

Women and men: life expectancy and the sex ratio at birth come from the World Bank (2023). The gender pay gap comes from the OECD (median full-time pay in Japan, the US, the UK, Germany, Mexico and Brazil), Singapore's Ministry of Manpower, Malaysia's DOSM and ILOSTAT for the rest. The motherhood penalty comes from The Child Penalty Atlas (Kleven, Landais and Leite-Mariante, 2025). China's pay gap (no official figure, so the ILO world average), Singapore's child penalty, pay gaps below zero (counted as zero) and turning the child penalty into lower pay are estimates.

Where you stand in the world: wealth ranks use the UBS Global Wealth Report 2023 and Global Wealth Databook 2023 (end of 2022, all 5.4 billion adults). Income ranks use the World Bank Poverty and Inequality Platform (2022, in 2021 PPP dollars). Game money is turned into US dollars with World Bank 2022 price levels. Ranks inside a country, wealth ranks below the world median, and income ranks are estimates.

Please note

This is a game for learning and fun, not financial advice. Real lives are messier than any model.
