A life, one card at a time

Around 50 choices in a life, drawn from a deck of 95, plus a few cards that belong to the country you’re born in. Each one touches your money, and often more than money.

No two lives draw the same hand, from your first pocket money to your will. Here are 20 of the cards. Time pauses for each one. Every year you also choose a focus, which quietly pays, or costs, for as long as you keep it.

Where the numbers come from

Wages, house prices, university fees and life expectancy are rough figures based on public World Bank and UN data. All money is in dollars adjusted to local prices (PPP) and kept in today's money, so inflation is already taken out.

In the game, stocks grow about 4–5% a year after inflation on average, through real crashes, booms and the occasional lost decade.

Women and men: life expectancy and the sex ratio at birth come from the World Bank (2023). The gender pay gap comes from the OECD (median full-time pay in Japan, the US, the UK, Germany, Mexico and Brazil), Singapore's Ministry of Manpower, Malaysia's DOSM and ILOSTAT for the rest. The motherhood penalty comes from The Child Penalty Atlas (Kleven, Landais and Leite-Mariante, 2025). China's pay gap (no official figure, so the ILO world average), Singapore's child penalty, pay gaps below zero (counted as zero) and turning the child penalty into lower pay are estimates.

Chances in a life: serious illness follows each country's causes of death by age and its chance of dying of heart disease, cancer, diabetes or lung disease between 30 and 70 (WHO Global Health Estimates, 2019 and 2021). Dating, marriage and family follow the average age at first marriage (UN World Marriage Data) and children per woman (UN World Population Prospects, 2024); layoffs follow unemployment (ILO, 2025); what cash loses follows inflation since 2000 (IMF); leaving school early and getting a university place follow school completion and university enrolment (UNESCO Institute for Statistics); road accidents follow road deaths (WHO, 2019); floods, storms, droughts and earthquakes follow the people affected by them in 2000–2024 (EM-DAT, via Our World in Data). Most come through the World Bank. Where a country's figure is missing or old, the World Bank's average for its region is used. Every number, with its source and year, is in js/data.js; what a disaster or an accident costs is the game's own estimate.

Where you stand in the world: wealth ranks use the UBS Global Wealth Report 2023 and Global Wealth Databook 2023 (end of 2022, all 5.4 billion adults). Income ranks use the World Bank Poverty and Inequality Platform (2022, in 2021 PPP dollars). Game money is turned into US dollars with World Bank 2022 price levels. Ranks inside a country, wealth ranks below the world median, and income ranks are estimates.

A quiet note

This is a game for learning and play, not financial advice. Real lives are always messier than any model.
