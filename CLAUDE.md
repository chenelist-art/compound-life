# A Second Chance

A static website (no build step, no framework): a money life simulator inspired by 1in8b.life. The name is "A Second Chance" (Chinese 重来人生 / Taiwan 重來人生: `h.brand`, `h.title`, `h.gtitle`, `g.title`, `l.title`, `g.share` in `js/i18n.js`, plus the static English in the HTML). It used to be "Compound Life": the folder, the GitHub repo and the `cl-` localStorage keys keep the old name on purpose (renaming the keys would lose saved past lives). Banknote serials start with "SC". Open `index.html` in a browser to run it. Owner: Chen, a business student; keep explanations in plain language.

## Structure
- `index.html` – landing page (hero banknote, money lab, birth map, decision list). Logic in `js/landing.js`.
- `play.html` – the game. Logic in `js/game.js`.
- `js/engine.js` – the simulation: country table `C`, decision deck `EV` (95 decisions), yearly `tick()`, passbook `trkView()`, and `projectEvent()` / `projectFocus()` (40 simulated futures per choice).
  - Each life is dealt its own mix: `planLife()` gives every event with `win:[from,to]` an age in that window, with chance `p` (scaled by `DENSITY`, about 50 decisions a life) and only if `only(s)` allows it at birth (country or family). `when(s)` is checked at the time. After two quiet years `pullForward()` brings the nearest open card forward (from the plan, then from `spare`), so something happens every year or two. No decision repeats in a life.
  - Consequences that arrive years later use `later(s, age, kind, data)`, resolved by `LATER[kind]` at the start of that year (friend repays, bubble bursts, divorce, layoffs, emergencies…). Draw their randomness with `evR(s, key)` so the 40 simulated futures see different outcomes.
  - Every life has a sex (`s.sex`, `f` or `m`), chosen on the setup screen or drawn from the country's sex ratio at birth. It shifts lifespan and, for women, pay (`sexPay`: the pay gap, plus the child penalty for ten years after a first child), shown as two passbook lines. Death uses one lifetime luck draw (`s.dieAt`) against accumulated yearly risk, so `mirrorLife()` (the same life as the other sex, shown on the end card) compares fairly.
  - Relationships (`s.rel`, 0–100) is the fourth stat. Options that affect people use `OR(id, label, sub, d, go, ok, hint)`, which moves it by `d` and shows the change only in Easy. It drifts down each year unless the focus is People or Balance, and it feeds promotions, referrals (`refer()`), marriage and divorce odds, loan repayments (`relP()`), help when sick or broke (`helped()`) and loneliness in retirement.
  - Love and family (game rules, not data; all in the block above `smallKids`): no fixed wedding or children card. While single (18–70) each year has a chance to meet someone (`meetP()`: lower with age, higher with Relationships, the People focus or a year spent looking); the `date` card ("You met someone", comes back with `again`) then asks to start dating or stay single. The partner is `s.pt` (`{n, st:'date'|'live'|'wed', car, q, exp, at, from, nw0, tk}`, never "he" or "she"); `s.married` stays in step. Everything after that is the player's: the Life choices panel calls `relActs(s)` (what makes sense now, each `{id, ok}`) and `relAct(s, id, L)`: `look`, `move` (live together without marrying), `wed` (sets `s.ask`, which brings up the `wed` card at once), `baby` (`s.tryAt`; `babyP(age)` decides, the child is born the next year), `break`, `div`. Partners have their own career (`ptCar()`: weights in `PTJ` between a poorer and a richer country) and pay (`ptPay()`); living together or married, `ptYear()` adds their take-home pay to the household and their living costs (same as yours), with a passbook line `pt1`, `pt2`… Dating costs a little (`date1`… lines). Dating couples split more often than those living together (`splitUp()`); marriages end through the old money-fights card and `LATER.div`, or the player's Divorce: `divorce()` pays legal fees and child support by country (`DIVC`, estimates) and splits half of what was built since the wedding (`nw0`). Break-ups and divorces cost Happiness that fades (`heal()`); staying single gives a little Happiness under 40 and Relationships drift down after 60 unless you invest in people. Every panel action is kept in `s.acts` and every card pick by age in `s.hist`, so `mirrorLife()` replays them. Lives nobody steers (`s.auto`: the twin, the 40 futures, the peers, the balance bots) follow `autoRel()`: marry after a couple of years, a child or two before 40.
  - Health events (game rules, not published data; tuned in `ILLT`, `illP()`, `survP()`): a serious illness (`ill`: heart attack, stroke or cancer, once a life) gets likelier as Health falls below 50 and then 30, with age, and with grind/hustle years (`s.grindY`, `s.hustleYears`). Its card offers best hospital / standard / no treatment; insurance pays 85%. Survival is rolled in `LATER.ill` (Health, treatment, age, screening, country life expectancy); death ends the life (`s.illDeath`), survivors lose months of pay and can't build Health while recovering (`sick(s)`). Depression (`dep`) can follow two years of Happiness under 35: pay −30%, fewer promotions, Health falls, focus works half as well (`depressed(s)`). Both show as badges on the net-worth card and as passbook lines. Events with `surv:true` get a survival share (`alive`) in `projectEvent()`, shown before money in projections and hindsight. Keep the wording calm and non-graphic; never mention suicide or self-harm; the depression card keeps its `care` line. With random choices about 16% of lives end from illness on Easy and 20% on Normal and Hard (about 1% for the smart bot, which stays healthy and always takes the best treatment); re-check this with `tools/balance.js` after changing the odds.
  - Careers (`CAR`, 31 careers in five families: office, hands-on, creative, sports, own path) replace the old three job offers. Pay is `base × carR() × Skill/level/experience`, where `r` comes from US BLS OEWS medians (May 2025, ÷ the all-jobs median) and `carR()` adjusts it per country with ILOSTAT earnings by ISCO-08 group (`ISCO`); sources and estimates are listed in the comment above `ISCO`. Each career has requirements (`req`: degree, Skill, Health, age, savings; `carLock()` gives the reason shown on a greyed-out option) and its own risks: layoffs (higher in the crash year), automation from 45, injuries, sports careers ending (`end`), creative and sports pay tiers, businesses that fail or grow (`own`), commission and market swings. The career card (`job`) comes back whenever a career ends (`endCareer()` sets `needJob` and clears `done.job`); the "Switch careers?" card (`switch`) comes every 6–10 years and the year after a layoff or injury (`offerSwitch()`). Cards that can repeat clear themselves with `again`. Switching keeps half the experience in the same family, a fifth in a new one. Each career is one passbook line (`car1`, `car2`… "Footballer, 18–31"). Projections for long cards run one option at a time (`projBegin()` / `projNext()`). Re-check career spread and the best typical career per card with a Node sim after changing pay or risks.
  - Yearly costs you sign up for (pets, premiums, supporting parents) use `rec(s, id, amount, years)`; a negative amount is income. Pay changes (raises, a job abroad, going part-time) use `bump()`.
  - Difficulty changes the rules, not just the hints (`DF`, read with `D(s)`; each life keeps it as `s.dk`, the 4th argument of `newLife()`, and the twin, the mirror life and the peers use the same). Easy keeps the original rules. Normal and Hard add: pay that grows less, extra tax on high pay, lifestyle creep, rents rising faster than pay, more medical bills and layoffs, surprise bills (`shock`, one "Unexpected bills" passbook line), a lost decade for stocks (`s.lostAt`), crashes of different depths (`s.crashM`), degrees/courses/Learn that teach more or less (`learnLuck()`), medical and law exams you can fail (`trainFail`), a Happiness cost for saving a lot (`frug`), a lower point where money stops making you happier (`wcap`) and long-hours jobs that cost more Happiness, Health and time with people (`toll`). The table's comment lists every knob; all are game rules, not data.
  - The end grade (`lifeScore()`, weights and cut-offs in `GR`): Money, Happiness, Health (with lifespan) and Relationships count a quarter each, with diminishing returns, from yearly sums in `s.acc` kept by `tick()`. The end card shows the letter (S/A/B/C/D) and the four parts; past lives store it as `g`.
  - You can't choose what you can't pay for: `evOpts()` greys out an option (`g.cantPay`) when cash, reachable savings and what the bank will still lend (`debtCap()`: two years of a typical wage) don't cover it; the default is never greyed out. Costs you can't avoid past the limit mean going without: Happiness falls, and `s.acc.gw` counts it against the Money grade (unpaid interest too). Use `evOpts()` wherever options are listed for the player.
  - Retirement accounts (401(k), EPF, CPF top-ups) are locked until 60/55 or retiring (`lock` on the lot, `locked()`); `eqTot(s, true)` and `liquid()` leave them out. Training fees and the shortfall while training go on the student loan.
- Life choices panel (in `js/game.js`: `buildLife()`, `updLife()`, `lifeAct()`): under the focus cards from 1200px wide, a tab on top of the bottom bar below that, opening a small sheet. Shows the status (single, dating, living together, married, the partner's job and pay, children) and only the actions `relActs()` allows; buttons stay greyed out while a card or moment is open and are only rebuilt when they change. Break up and Divorce need a second tap within 4 s, and the years stop while they wait (`G.lcArm`). Each action goes into `G.dec` as `p_<id>` for the replay (`p_move` under Love, `p_baby` under Children when a baby came of it, `p_div`/`p_break` under Hard times) and opens a moment (dating, together, married, trying, child, breakup, left, divorce); the partner walks in or away on those cards (`pmove` in `js/pixel.js`). Hints (`lc.<id>.h`) show in Easy only.
- Yearly focus panel (in `js/game.js`: `buildFocus()`, `updFocus()`): five small cards (drawings are `Art.focusWin()`) always on screen while a life runs: a sticky left column from 1200px wide, a fixed row of chips along the bottom below that. A pick waits in `G.nextF` and becomes `s.focus` at the start of the next year in `advance()` (from 18; disabled once retired). It shows no money forecasts, in any difficulty.
- Life replay (in `js/game.js`, before the end card and from its "Watch my life again" button): `choose()` records every decision in `G.dec` with the passbook lines it started and every option's typical result from the 40 futures (`rows`, relative to the default option, plus `tgt`). `replayPages()` picks one page per life stage that happened (`RP`: the stage's main decision, or the biggest by passbook value; up to two "hard times"), plus birth, retirement and the last page, sorted by age. The pages are drawn as one continuous charcoal line in an inline SVG (`rpWorld()`): each scene is `Art.stageArt()` (the stage drawing without its ground) drawn twice, a line layer whose strokes draw themselves one by one (`pathLength="1"`, `stroke-dashoffset` animated with the Web Animations API in `rpDraw()`) and a fill layer of flat palette colours that fades in afterwards; the ground between scenes is the net-worth line, a yellow pen dot rides on the stroke being drawn and the view pans to follow it (`rpFrame()`). About 2.5 s a scene; tapping the drawing speeds it up, a scene you return to shows finished (`rpShow()`), reduced motion shows every scene finished. Each scene's words fade in beside it (below it on phones): a short story (`decStory()`: what you chose, then what its passbook lines did, e.g. "cost $15k at first, then paid you back by 36") and "the road not taken" (`roadHTML()`: the other options compared with yours, best one marked). The player moves on with the buttons, swipe or ←/→ (Esc or Skip ends it). After the last scene the line slows down and draws a small star (`rpFinale()`), then the screen fades to charcoal for "Was it all worth it?" in washi white (the one dark-background exception in `design/picture-book-style.md`), holds ~3 s, then fades back to light into the end card. Reduced motion: no drawing or fades, the question just shows for 3 s. Stage drawings are `STAGE` in `js/art.js` (`Art.stageWin()` for cards, `Art.stageArt()` for the replay); text keys are `rp.*`.
- Big moment cards (in `js/game.js`, after `endLife()`): a tarot flashcard that flips in over the faded game and stops time until Continue / Space / Enter. Diary lines listed in `MOMENT` open one (laid off, business failed, crypto crash, property bubble, broke, divorce, sports injury, promoted, graduated, business took off, startup sold, baby born, retired, moved in together, broke up, partner left, trying for a baby); `lifeMoments()` spots a new partner, a wedding, a new job and the last mortgage payment from the state; the crash, illness and inheritance decisions (`MO_EV`) open with their moment first. Death shows a farewell card (cause told gently: `mo.die.*`) before the replay. Each card: title `mo.<kind>.t`, the diary line (or `mo.<kind>.x`), a money line from `moMoney()` (`mo.n.*`, `{a}` signed, `{b}` without the sign), a drawing from `Art.momentWin()` and the pixel character in a matching pose (`moPose()`). Promotions within three years of the last promotion card, and the small events in `SMALL`, are toasts. The setting "Big moment cards: on / off" is saved as `cl-moments`; off turns every moment into a toast.
- `js/pixel.js` – the chibi pixel character (a 48×48 scene on a canvas, scaled by a whole number with `image-rendering: pixelated`, CSS `--px`): head about half the height, tiny round body, stubby arms and legs, a green sprout on the player's head. `Pixel.stateOf(s)` reads a life (age stage, career outfit from `OUT`, partner, children's ages, wealth, house, car, mood from Happiness (happy ≥70), illness, depression, money trouble `broke`, `retired`); `Pixel.scene(state, frame)` builds it and `Pixel.set(canvas, state)` keeps it animated (squash-and-stretch, blinking, a head tilt now and then). Faces (`EYE`, `face()`): happy ^ ^, normal sparkly eyes, sad teary, sick dizzy, sleepy for elders, calm closed eyes once dead. Emotes over the head: a rain cloud (depression), sweat drop (`broke`), zzz (retired); `Pixel.cheer(canvas, kind)` jumps with hearts (`'love'`: wedding, new baby) or sparkles (`'yay'`: graduation, promotion, new ring, career steps: `CHEER` in game.js). Walking children hold a parent's hand (two at most), babies are carried, more children show as "+N". Only the six palette colours; each figure gets one 1px charcoal outline. It is on the net-worth card, on each replay page (`G.pix` keeps the look per age) and big on the end card. A new career needs an entry in `OUT` (one or two signature items). `preview-character.html` shows every age, face, emote, outfit and family on one page; check it after changing the character.
- `js/i18n.js` – all text in English (`EN`), Simplified Chinese (`ZH`) and Traditional Chinese (`ZHT`), plus money formatting (`fm`): $12k / $1.2万 / $1.2萬.
- `js/guilloche.js` – canvas rosettes drawn as picture-book suns and moons (`Guilloche.SUN`, `Guilloche.MOON`). `js/art.js` – tarot card pieces (`Art.card`, `Art.decorate`, Roman numerals) and one small SVG drawing per decision. `js/theme.js` – `fitText` (shrinks big numbers to fit).
- `css/style.css` – the whole design system, following `design/picture-book-style.md`. Colours are the six palette tokens on `:root`; never hard-code colours in components. Light theme only: no dark mode, no gradients, no blurred shadows.

## Rules
- **No hard-coded user-facing text in JS.** The engine stores messages as `M(key, vars)` and money as `MN(n)` / `MS(n)` (signed); the UI turns them into text with `t()` / `tr()` at render time, so switching language mid-game works.
- Adding or changing text means updating all three languages in `js/i18n.js` (`EN`, `ZH`, `ZHT`). Traditional Chinese uses Taiwan wording.
- Static HTML text uses `data-i18n="h.key"`; the English in the HTML is the English version, and `ZH`/`ZHT` need the `h.key` entry.
- Money is in PPP dollars, in today's money (inflation already removed). Stocks average ~5% real with yearly noise; on Normal and Hard some lives also get a lost decade and deeper crashes.
- "Where you stand" ranks (`worldPct`, `countryPct`, `incomePct` in `js/engine.js`) use real published data in `WEALTH_PCT`, `INCOME_PCT`, `WD` and `PPP`, each with its source and year in a comment (UBS Global Wealth Report / Databook 2023, World Bank PIP and WDI). The same rule covers the women-and-men tables `LE_SEX`, `SRB`, `PAYGAP` and `CHILDPEN` (World Bank WDI, OECD, MOM Singapore, DOSM Malaysia, ILOSTAT, The Child Penalty Atlas), whose sources are listed in the footer text `h.f2p4`. Never add or change a number there without a source; anything modelled rather than published (ranks inside a country, below the world median, income vs pay) must stay labelled as an estimate, in the code and in the footer text `h.f2p3`.
- Keep it working at phone width (390px) with no sideways scrolling.
- Fonts come from Google Fonts: Huninn for everything, with Noto Sans SC as the fallback for Simplified Chinese.
- Gains and losses are shown with a marker underline (`.gain` / `.loss`), never with coloured text.
- Every decision in `EV` needs a tarot name `tn.<id>` in all three languages and a drawing in `js/art.js`. The 78-card deck is used except The Moon (`tn.kids`, free since children became a Life choice), so a new decision can take The Moon or has to reuse a card name.
- Every decision's default option (`def`) must always be clickable, or the autopilot twin and the game can get stuck.
- Difficulty (setup screen, saved as `cl-diff`): **Easy** (the original rules) shows the 40-future projections before you choose, **Normal** (default, harder rules) shows a hindsight card for a few seconds after you choose with only your own choice's result, **Hard** (harsher still) shows nothing until the end card. The end card lists every decision's hindsight, every option, in all modes.
- An option's `sub` (and an event's `text`) may only say what you'd know in real life: prices, pay, fees. Effects you couldn't know (skill gains, pay %, success odds) go in `hint` (`Hn(...)` on options, `hint:` on events), which only Easy shows.

## Checking changes
- Run a local server from this folder: `python -m http.server 8000`, then open http://localhost:8000.
- After engine changes, play at least one full life in each language and check the console for errors and for any raw keys like `e.car.t` showing on screen. With ~50 decisions a life, it also helps to simulate many lives in Node (load `js/i18n.js` then `js/engine.js` into one `vm` context, pick random options, `tick()` until death) and check for errors, stuck lives, repeats and untranslated text.
- Balance test: `node tools/balance.js` plays 500 lives on each difficulty with a smart bot (invests, studies, insures, never gambles, otherwise cheapest; best-paid steady career), a random bot, a default bot and a mixed bot (each life has its own care level); bots never use the Life choices panel, so their love lives follow `autoRel()` (`s.auto`), and reports net worth, happiness, health, relationships, grades, survival, serious setbacks and smart-vs-random. `node tools/balance.js normal 300 opt` also plays every option of every decision to the end of the same life (same luck) and reports how often each option was the best by life grade; it takes a few minutes. `ENGINE=path` runs another copy of the engine to compare. Re-run it after changing costs, odds or the grade.
